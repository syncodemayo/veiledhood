How it works

Four steps, one
private transaction.

Heavy encrypted computation runs off-chain where it's feasible. The chain keeps doing what public chains are good at.

STEP 01
Deposit
Value enters the shielded pool and becomes a note. Nothing about it is publicly readable.
STEP 02
Commit
The note is committed to a Merkle tree. The tree proves the note exists without revealing which one it is.
STEP 03
Prove
Spending proves membership and burns a nullifier, so a note can never be spent twice.
STEP 04
Settle
The contract verifies and settles on Robinhood Chain — publicly checkable, privately sourced.

Questions

The short answers.

What makes this different from other privacy tools?
Most privacy products hide a single action. VeiledHood covers the whole workflow — swaps, balances, bridging, data and agent activity — because a profile gets assembled from ordinary use, not from one transaction.
Can VeiledHood read my data?
No. Encryption happens on your device before anything is uploaded, and your key never leaves it. The server stores opaque blobs and can see sizes and timestamps — not contents.
Does the chain still verify my transactions?
Yes. Commitment roots, nullifiers, proof verification and settlement all happen on-chain and are publicly checkable. What stays private is the link between you and the note being spent.
What does "shielded" actually mean here?
Your holding becomes a note inside a pool of 24,180 others. Spending it proves the note exists and hasn't been spent, without revealing which note it is.
What do agents cost?
Pay-per-call in USDC with no account or API key — 0.01 for private inference, 0.005 for a wallet context read, 0.002 for an encrypted data fetch. Prices are model-specific rather than a flat fee.
Do I have to shield everything?
No. Shielded and public balances sit side by side, and you choose per action. Shield-by-default is on at setup and can be turned off in Settings.
